This report establishes standard measures of effect for Vietnam's distinctive media ecology. Key findings:

- **Smaller creators have more influence.** Content creators with 2,000–20,000 followers (nano- and micro-influencers) are more likely to engage audiences than large or very large accounts; mega-influencers (>1 million followers) retain influence through scale alone, while mid-sized creators have plateaued.
- **Expertise matters, especially locally.** When a local influencer displays high expertise in a topic, participants are five times more likely to give higher trust ratings and nearly three times more likely to report an intention to act on that advice.
- **Vietnam has a distinct media ecology.** Its blend of national and international platforms, geographic and age diversity, and high connectivity produce a communication environment unlike any other country's.
- **Misinformation and cultural knowledge are easily conflated.** Media systems in Vietnam carry the country's knowledge systems across journalism, culture, health and other public-interest areas; there is a risk of pathologising cultural beliefs as misinformation, and clear boundaries between local custom and scientific misinformation are needed.
- **Mental health is a critical focus area.** Of all the expertise explored, mental health requires the most attention across the media sector, as generational attitudes shift and digital resources emerge rapidly.
- **Trust is contested in a hybrid environment.** Vietnam's mix of traditional and digital media, with differing editorial and governance approaches, creates tension between trusting content creators and trusting the sources of their information.
